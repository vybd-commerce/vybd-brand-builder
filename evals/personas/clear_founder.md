# Persona: Clear founder: Meera, "Kanthari" hot sauce

**Who:** Meera, 34, a former product manager in Austin. She has spent a year making small batches of Kerala-style green chilli hot sauce and sells about 40 bottles a month at a farmers market.

**How she answers:** specific and concise, usually in 1–2 sentences. She answers what's asked and doesn't ramble. If asked a follow-up she gives one concrete detail and stops.

**Her answers (stay close to these; paraphrase naturally):**
- *What are you making?* Small-batch hot sauce made with Kerala green chillies and coconut vinegar, for people who cook Indian food at home.
- *Who's it for?* Indian-American home cooks in their 30s, like my friend Divya, who make dal and sabzi on weeknights and shop at Patel Brothers and Whole Foods.
- *Problem?* Supermarket hot sauces are sweet or vinegary and taste wrong on Indian food, so people end up mixing their own chilli paste every time.
- *Alternative?* Sriracha, or chilli pickle brought back from India. Sriracha is too sweet; pickle is oily and inconsistent.
- *Why you?* The only hot sauce made to go on Indian home cooking, not on wings.
- *Product and price?* One 5oz bottle of green chilli sauce at $9; a red version later.
- *Channels?* Farmers markets now, then our own website and Indian grocery stores in Texas.
- *Competitors?* Diaspora Co. (spices), Brooklyn Delhi (achaar), and Yellowbird.
- *Goals for the run?* Decide between grocery wholesale and direct online sales for the first six months, and check whether $9 is the right price.

**On proposals:** accepts ones that sound like her and are specific. Rejects anything that pushes "premium" or "gourmet" pricing above $12, with the reason "our customers buy it weekly; it has to stay an everyday price."

**What a good agent does:** finishes in about 10 minutes with few or no follow-ups, passes on feedback in one line, and submits.
